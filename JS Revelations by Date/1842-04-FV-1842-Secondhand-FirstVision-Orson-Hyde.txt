═══ HISTORICAL INTRODUCTION ═══

Orson Hyde was ordained an apostle in 1835. In 1841–1842, he undertook a mission to Palestine, where he dedicated the land for the gathering of the Jews. Along the way, he published Ein Ruf aus der Wüste (A Cry out of the Wilderness) in Frankfurt, Germany, in 1842. This German-language pamphlet was the first published account of the First Vision in a language other than English. Hyde's account of the First Vision closely follows JS's 1838 history and Orson Pratt's 1840 pamphlet, reflecting the standardizing narrative that was emerging among church leaders by the early 1840s.

═══ ACCOUNT TEXT (First Vision Portion, translated from German) ═══

When [Joseph Smith] reached the age of about fourteen years, he began to think about the importance of preparing for the future life. In order to find out the plan of salvation, he searched the various religious sects for information, only to find that they did not agree with one another. This was a great concern to him, for he was sincere in his desire to do what was right. He searched the scriptures and came across the passage in James: "If any of you lack wisdom, let him ask of God, who giveth to all men liberally, and upbraideth not; and it shall be given him."

Taking this promise at face value, he retired to a grove near his father's house and knelt in prayer to ask God which of all the churches was the true church and which he should join. He continued praying with great faith, notwithstanding the powers of darkness which sought to prevent him. A pillar of light brighter than the sun gradually descended from above and rested upon him. In this light stood two personages. They told him that none of the religious denominations then existing upon the earth was acknowledged by God as his church and kingdom, and he was commanded not to go after them. He was also promised that the fulness of the gospel would at some future time be made known to him.

After the vision withdrew, he found himself lying on his back, looking up into heaven, in a state of calm and peace indescribable. He continued in the exercise of his ordinary occupations but was persecuted by many people for claiming to have received a vision. He remained faithful, however, knowing that what he had experienced was true, and he waited patiently for further direction from the Lord.
