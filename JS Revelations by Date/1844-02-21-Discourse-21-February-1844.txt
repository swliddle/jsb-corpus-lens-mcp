═══ HISTORICAL INTRODUCTION ═══

On 21 February 1844, JS delivered a discourse to a group of in , Illinois, emphasizing the importance of obeying God’s commandments. Earlier that evening, JS had attended a lecture by a Reverend DeWolf, an Episcopal priest, in the assembly room on the second floor of . Once DeWolf concluded, he requested that JS speak. JS then “spoke to the people to show them that to get salvation we must not only do some things but every thing whi[c]h God had comandd [commanded].”
As DeWolf was lecturing in the assembly room, members of the and others gathered in JS’s , which was also on the second floor of his . Apostle was present at the meeting in JS’s office, and once it had concluded he presumably heard at least some of JS’s closing remarks in the assembly room. Woodruff later recorded them in his journal. The quality of his handwriting suggests that the account in his journal was a fair copy. It is unknown, however, if Woodruff created his account from notes he took during JS’s remarks or whether he reconstructed it from memory.

═══ DISCOURSE TEXT ═══

Among other things said that men may preach & practice evry thing except those things which God commands us to do & will be damned at last we may tithe rue annis anise & cummin cumin & still not obey the commandments of God the object with me is to obey & teach others to obey God in just what he tells us to do It mattereth not whether the principle is popular or unpopular I will always maintain a true principl even if I stand alone in it [p. [195]]
