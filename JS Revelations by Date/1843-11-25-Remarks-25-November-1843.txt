═══ HISTORICAL INTRODUCTION ═══

On the evening of 25 November 1843, JS delivered remarks to the , Illinois, and members of the at the conclusion of the trial of member . Four days earlier, JS had preferred charges against Sagers for trying to seduce Phebe Madison, a young woman living in Sagers’s home. Sagers had evidently been teaching the doctrine of plural marriage prior to JS’s charges. On 25 November, the high council met in the upper room of to deliberate on this and several other cases. During the meeting, JS presented his charges against Sagers, and Sagers pleaded not guilty. After the appointed council members spoke on behalf of and against the defendant, the council ruled that the charge was “not sustained” and, as stated in JS’s journal, the high council took “no action” against Sagers.
Following the council’s verdict, JS told those gathered that he did not condone fornication or adultery and warned that those who used his name to justify engaging in such practices would be condemned. later recorded JS’s comments in his journal. This text appears to be the only detailed account of JS’s remarks. Following the trial, Sagers taught the doctrine of plural marriage and may have engaged in the practice.

President Joseph Smith made an address upon the Subject which was highly interesting & its tendency was to do away with evry evil & practice virtue & Holiness before the Lord that the had not received any license from him to commit adultery fornication or any such thing but to the contrary if any man commit adultery He could not receive the of God even if he was saved in any kingdom it could not be the celestial kingdom He said he thought the many examples that had been manifest & others was sufficient to show the fallacy of such a course of conduct, He condemned [p. [166]]

the principle in toto & warned those present against going into those evils, for they would shurely bring a Curse upon their heads [p. [167]]
