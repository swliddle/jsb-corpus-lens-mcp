Oliver Cowdery, "Letter VIII," Latter Day Saints' Messenger and Advocate, October 1835 — Vision at the Hill Cumorah

═══ SOURCE NOTE ═══
Source: Oliver Cowdery, "Letter VIII," Latter Day Saints' Messenger and Advocate 2, no. 1 (October 1835): 195–202.
As preserved in: JS History, 1834–1836, pages 89–102.
URL: https://www.josephsmithpapers.org/paper-summary/history-1834-1836/93

Oliver Cowdery wrote a series of eight letters to W. W. Phelps, published in the Latter Day Saints' Messenger and Advocate between October 1834 and October 1835, detailing the early history of the church and Joseph Smith's early experiences. These letters were composed with Joseph Smith's assistance and were later copied into JS's History, 1834–1836. Letter VIII describes Joseph Smith's first visit to the Hill Cumorah on 22 September 1823, including a vision in which the angel Moroni showed Joseph both the glory of God and the prince of darkness with his hosts.

═══ HISTORICAL INTRODUCTION ═══

On the evening of 21 September 1823, the angel Moroni appeared to Joseph Smith in his family's home near Palmyra, New York, informing him of the existence of an ancient record deposited in a nearby hill. The following day, 22 September 1823, Joseph went to the Hill Cumorah for the first time. According to Oliver Cowdery's account — written with Joseph Smith's participation — Joseph located the stone box containing the plates and, upon seeing the gold plates and other objects, experienced a powerful vision in which the angel showed him both the glory of God and the "prince of darkness, surrounded by his innumerable train of associates." This vision was given, the angel explained, so that Joseph would "know hereafter the two powers and never be influenced or overcome by that wicked one." The account also describes how Joseph's initial desire for worldly gain was corrected by the angel's stern reminder that the record was sacred and not to be used for personal enrichment.

This account was written by Oliver Cowdery in 1835 with Joseph Smith's direct involvement and was subsequently copied into JS's manuscript history. While the account is written in Cowdery's voice, it was understood by contemporaries to reflect Joseph Smith's own recollection of events.

═══ ACCOUNT TEXT ═══

On the morning of the 22d of September, 1823, he went to the place where the record was deposited, as the angel had directed.

On the west side of this hill, not far from the top, under a stone of considerable size, lay the record, in a box of stone. This hill is about two hundred feet above the plain at its base, and has a somewhat remarkable formation — its north end rising almost perpendicular for about sixty feet, being mostly covered with timber, and from that to the south it gradually declines to its southern extremity. To the west it is quite steep, though not so much so as on the north.

He repaired to the place, as had been directed. By removing the soil and moss from the edges of the stone, he was enabled to get a lever under it, and, with a little exertion, raised it. Beneath it lay the record, deposited in a box, in manner and form as the messenger had stated. While gazing at the contents of the box, Joseph was overwhelmed by the sight, and a desire for worldly wealth began to cloud his mind.

At this point, the heavenly messenger appeared and corrected him, reminding him that the record was written and deposited for sacred purposes, not for the accumulation of gain and wealth.

While he thus stood gazing and admiring, the angel said, "Look!" and as he thus spake he beheld the prince of darkness, surrounded by his innumerable train of associates. All this passed before him, and the heavenly messenger said, "All this is shown, the good and the evil, the holy and impure, the glory of God and the power of darkness, that you may know hereafter the two powers and never be influenced or overcome by that wicked one. Behold, whatever is calculated to lead thee astray, and to do wrong, thou wilt know it by the constraining of the spirit of the Lord; and that which is calculated to bring happiness, and that which leadeth to good and to do good, wilt thou know it by the same spirit. All things which are good cometh of God, and that which is evil cometh of the devil. Now, learn this, once and for all, and remember the words which I have told thee. This darkness which you have beheld is of Satan, and his power extends over all those things which are not of God. He will try to overthrow you, and he will seek to lead the people who shall follow you astray. And when you have the record, whatever course you may be required to pursue, be true to the Lord. Walk uprightly before him; and if ever you should be tempted to do wrong, remember this vision; remember that God is the fountain of all good, and that Satan is the source of all evil; and remember that if you do right, no power can stay the work of God; and that if you do not right, he will raise up another in your place; for the work shall go on, for it is the Lord's."

The messenger also told him that the record contained sacred things — that it was an abridgment of the history of a people who once lived upon this continent and that the fulness of the gospel was contained therein as delivered by the Savior. The angel further informed Joseph that he was not to obtain the record at that time but should return to the same place on the 22d of September of the following year, and continue to do so until the time should come for obtaining the plates.
