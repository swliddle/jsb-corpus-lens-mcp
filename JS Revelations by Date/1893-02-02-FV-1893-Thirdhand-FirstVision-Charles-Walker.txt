On 2 February 1893, Alger shared this recollection at a fast meeting in St. George, Utah. Charles L. Walker recorded Alger's words in his diary that same day. This account is notable for a unique physical detail not found in any other account: that God the Father "touched his [Joseph's] eyes with his finger" before Joseph could see the Savior, and that Joseph illustrated this by putting "his finger to his right eye" while speaking.

═══ HISTORICAL INTRODUCTION ═══

Charles Lowell Walker (1832–1904) was a British convert who settled in St. George, Utah, and kept a remarkably detailed diary spanning decades of frontier church life. John Alger was a young boy living in Kirtland, Ohio, in the mid-1830s who personally heard Joseph Smith relate the First Vision in the Smith family home. Alger's recollection, shared publicly at a fast meeting on 2 February 1893, was recorded by Walker in his diary the same day. The account is significant as the only known version to describe a physical act of God — touching Joseph's eyes with His finger — as a prerequisite to seeing the Savior. Joseph reportedly illustrated the detail with a physical gesture, putting his finger to his right eye. Walker notes that the detail "was something that we had never seen in church history or heard of before."

Source: Karl Larson and Katharine Miles Larson, eds., Diary of Charles Lowell Walker (Logan, UT: Utah State University Press, 1980), 2:755–56.

═══ ACCOUNT TEXT ═══

[2nd Feb Thurs 1893, Fast Meeting, St. George, Utah]

Br John Alger said while speaking of the Prophet Joseph, that when he, John, was a small boy he heard the Prophet Joseph relate his vision of seeing The Father and the Son, That God touched his eyes with his finger and said "Joseph this is my beloved Son hear him." As soon as the Lord had touched his eyes with his finger he immediately saw the Savior.

After meeting, a few of us questioned him about the matter and he told us at the bottom of the meeting house steps that he was in the House of Father Smith in Kirtland when Joseph made this declaration, and that Joseph while speaking of it put his finger to his right eye, suiting the action with the words so as to illustrate and at the same time impress the occurrence on the minds of those unto whom He was speaking.

We enjoyed the conversation very much, as it was something that we had never seen in church history or heard of before.
