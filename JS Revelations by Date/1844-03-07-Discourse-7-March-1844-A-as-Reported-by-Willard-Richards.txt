═══ HISTORICAL INTRODUCTION ═══

On 7 March 1844, at the site where the was being built in , Illinois, JS delivered a discourse about recent challenges to the temple’s progress and city’s development. Three days earlier, JS had called the meeting, which noted was attended by “six or eight thousand saints.” JS gave two discourses, separated by sermons by several other members.
In JS’s first discourse of the meeting, he presented three specific examples of recent conflict caused by citizens. He briefly referenced , who had allegedly accused of having multiple “spiritual wives” and had claimed that many of Nauvoo’s females lived as prostitutes. JS then spoke of and , who were longtime landowners in the area and whose intervention in collecting wharfage from steamboat operators had occupied the city council two days earlier. He concluded by accusing local physician of writing an antagonistic, anonymous letter that had appeared in the New-York Daily Tribune at the end of January 1844.
, who was JS’s “private se[c]retary & historian,” recorded the discourse in JS’s journal, which he was keeping for JS. recorded the discourse in his own journal. Woodruff inadvertently duplicated a few phrases, which suggests that he was copying the text from another source, presumably rough notes that he made while at the meeting. In addition to the accounts by Richards and Woodruff, one of the principal targets of JS’s criticism——made an account. On 12 April 1844, Foster wrote a letter to , editor of the Warsaw Signal, in which he acerbically summarized the meeting. All three accounts of the discourse are featured here.
