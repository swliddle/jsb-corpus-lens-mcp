═══ HISTORICAL INTRODUCTION ═══

David Nye White was the editor of the Pittsburgh Weekly Gazette. He visited Nauvoo, Illinois, in late August 1843 and interviewed Joseph Smith. The interview was published in the Pittsburgh Weekly Gazette on 12 September 1843. White's published account includes a retelling of JS's early history, including his first vision of Deity. This account is distinctive as a journalist's rendering of JS's narrative, filtered through the editorial lens of a non-Mormon newspaper editor.

═══ ACCOUNT TEXT ═══

[From the Pittsburgh Weekly Gazette, 12 September 1843]
When he was about fourteen years old, he began to reflect upon the importance of being prepared for a future state of existence; in order to which, he perceived that it was necessary to know how to be saved. He found that there were several religious denominations in the neighborhood, each of which had its own peculiar views in regard to the plan of salvation, and that each one was pointing to its own particular tenets as the path in which to walk.— This contrariety of sentiment was a source of much concern to him, and he finally determined to settle the matter by an appeal to the word of God. He was directed, in reading the Scriptures, to the passage in James, in which it is written—"If any man lack wisdom, let him ask of God, who giveth to all men liberally;" and being convinced of the truth of this promise, he retired to the woods to pray. While thus engaged, supplicating Almighty God for wisdom and direction, a light appeared in the heavens, and descended until it rested upon the trees where he was. It appeared like fire. But to his great astonishment, did not burn the trees. An angel then appeared to him, and conversed with him upon many things. He was told that none of the sects were right; but that if he was faithful in keeping the commandments he should receive, the true way should be made known to him: that his sins were forgiven, &c.
